# The Cairn manifesto

*This is the edited edition of the owner's statement of 2026-09-02. It says
what the original says, in cleaner sentences; the
[verbatim original](./docs/cairn/manifesto.md) is kept beside the audit that
applied it, and where the two differ, the original is the source.*

## What defines the protocol

Cairn is the most lightweight and minimalistic harness-agnostic,
document-driven coding protocol.

## The engine

It is built on the norms of a knowledge base kept as files — one idea per
file, every folder navigable from its index — with three commitments:
optimised, distributed, decentralised.

The repository tree must be the leanest:

- no duplicate folders with close missions — the fewer, the better;
- a hierarchy whose logic stands on its own — accessibility by design;
- the lightest token footprint through exploration and navigation.

## The brain

At its core the protocol rests on a shared concept knowledge base, read by
agents and people alike, in three scopes: the protocol's own concepts, the
project's, and the abstractions in the code that carry complexity. We believe
that semantic decomposition — one idea, one page — is the best way to learn.

## The chronology

- You get an idea overnight. Instead of coding it right away, you sit on it.
- Through ideation, research and brainstorms, you refine your vision.
- From that vision you design a minimum product and write its specification
  documents: features, architecture, workflow, interfaces, contracts.
- From that set of documents you choose the best projected roadmap to the
  minimum product.
- From that roadmap you build each coding cycle around the documentation
  produced before it.
- During the project, when an idea arrives and you want to explore it, you
  research and document it the same way, to be referenced in the next cycles.

Your focus is on your vision and how it evolves. The protocol's role is not
to assume the vision is fixed but to invite you to challenge it, iteration by
iteration.

## The approach

The protocol must be in symbiosis with modern tools and best practices: the
most fluid and logical interaction with the workflows that already exist —
parallel agentic coding, version control, continuous integration and
delivery.

The core idea is to find the right balance between robustness, stability and
simplicity.

If the workflow needs a solution that is not native to those tools, it is most
probably not a best practice — and we specifically want to build on best
practices.

## The code

We must provide the safest and soundest coding workflow, inspired by the
state of the art:

- sequence isolated steps: planning, coding, reviewing, testing;
- think with an experienced and reflective mind: what is the simplest and
  cleanest way to implement this?
- adapt to constant change in the coding ecosystem: observe, assess and
  absorb the changes that drive progress.

## The engagement

Even though the protocol leverages production-grade tools and workflows, it
must be accessible on the surface. We provide the documentation to get used
to it, and the interface must be beginner-friendly.

## The pedagogy

The protocol must establish a culture of sharing through pedagogy. Every
written line — in the documentation, in the chat sessions, in the artefacts;
everything written or generated — must be written with the intention of being
effortlessly comprehensible.

## The project

Beyond documentation, the protocol maintains the lightest but most efficient
layer of project context. Its mission is to extract the essence of a project
well enough to be completely agnostic of any previous or future implementation
context.

## Main threats

- Searching for more control in the volume of tests and the complexity of
  workflows.
- Assuming that a state of the protocol is definitive and cannot be challenged
  by refactoring and simplification.
- Not embracing progress made outside the protocol.
- Not respecting the manifesto.

## Main opportunities

- Always be open to simpler workflows.
- Always challenge the protocol.
- Always provide meaning to what you build.
- Always leverage what you have built.
