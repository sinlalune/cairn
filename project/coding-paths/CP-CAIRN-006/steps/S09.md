# CP-CAIRN-006 S09

### S09 — one meaning for one glob — **COMPLETE**

#### Plan

The closing request's reviewer found that the two readers of a `writes:`
pattern disagree about what `**` means, and that the disagreement loses
an overlap.

`globToRegExp` compiled `**/` to `.*`, which swallows part of a name:
`a/**/x.md` matched `a/zzzx.md`. `patternsMeet` had always read `**` as
whole segments, which is what every glob means by it. So
`patternsMeet('a/**/x.md', 'a/*/*ax.md')` answered *no* while both
patterns name `a/a/ax.md` — two live paths could declare surfaces that
share a file and `writes-overlap` would say nothing. The rule that
reports a race was blind to one shape of race.

`**/` is zero or more COMPLETE segments now, in the one matcher both
rules use, so `scope-drift` and `writes-overlap` mean the same thing by a
declaration. The exhaustive agreement test that proved this property held
was written without `**` in its alphabet — that omission is what left the
hole, and it is where the fix is proved.

**The candidate is void.** Implementation changed after `C = 6828a7e`, so
the record returns to `running` and a new candidate is produced, as the
close skill and the repair table both say. The checker refused the change
itself, in the words S07 gave it: *the candidate is void; return the
record to `running`, produce a new candidate and repeat review and
acceptance on it.*

Definition-of-done item advanced: the fifth, which was not met — the
advisory did not name every pair of live paths whose surfaces meet.

- **`**/` is zero or more complete segments.** One line of `globToRegExp`:
  where `**` is followed by a separator it compiles to `(?:[^/]+\/)*` rather
  than `.*`, so it spans separators — the file's own words for it — without
  eating half a name. `**` at the end of a pattern still means everything
  below, which is the only shape this repository's own declarations use.
- **The agreement test covers `**`.** It exhausted every pair of patterns over
  a seven-segment alphabet and asserted that no file sits in both surfaces
  while the two are said not to meet — with `**` left out of the alphabet, and
  its name said so. `**` is in it now, and the reviewer's own example is named
  beneath it so a reader meets the case directly rather than trusting the
  sweep.
