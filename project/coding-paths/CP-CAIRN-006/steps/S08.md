# CP-CAIRN-006 S08

### S08 — the review rule and the fixture sweep — **COMPLETE**

#### Plan

The last two rules-facing records, and what makes every rule's fixture
prove what it claims.

**ADR-017 decision 2, the rule `review`.** A unit has five movements, and
the fourth leaves a record: `#### Review` in the step file, between the
self-review and the verification, one line per finding with its
disposition. The checker reads that section's presence in the step record
of the path's **current** unit — where `work-unit` already reads the
`cairn-unit` block — and refuses a unit that lacks it or carries it
empty. It reads presence, never judgement: whether the reader was fresh
and whether the dispositions are honest is what the owner reads at the
candidate. The `closure` type carries no step file and no review.

**ADR-004 decision 4, the fixture sweep.** A fixture whose range holds
only this path's commits cannot show the fault decision 3 names, so every
blocking rule's fixture contains a merged trunk commit carrying another
path's completed unit. The three path-branch harnesses — `pathRepository`,
`publishedRepository`, `readyRepository` — gain it at the root rather than
fixture by fixture, so a fixture written later has the shape without being
told. The corpus fixtures on a fresh installation have no path branch and
no range; that is named in *Tried and rejected* rather than papered over.

**Then what the tools say about themselves**: the catalogue and the
linkage regenerated, `tools/soundness.md` carrying the rules this path
added, and `docs/modules/application.md` describing the tools as they
are at the candidate, with no history.

Definition-of-done items advanced: the eighth, the ninth's second half,
and the seventh's fixture clause.

Type `implementation`: the failing test comes first.

Deliberately not in this unit: the `cairn-unit` skill's fifth movement,
chapter 5 and the step template, which ADR-017 decision 2 also names —
path 1 wrote all three, and item 10 requires `skills/` and `spec/index.md`
unchanged here.

- **`review`, blocking.** `reviewSection(text)` reads `#### Review` through
  `resolveScopeSection` and returns its body, `''` for a heading with nothing
  under it — the shape a writer leaves when the movement was skipped and the
  template copied — and `null` for no section at all. The rule reads the step
  file of the unit whose `step` is the record's `current_step`, where
  `work-unit` already reads that unit's other claims, and refuses both empty
  shapes with a message naming the two halves of the remedy: hand the diff to a
  fresh context, then write it into *that* step. `closure` writes no step file
  and carries no review.
- **A checker defect the sweep uncovered.** `git log --follow` re-runs rename
  detection to carry a record across a move, and two step records of the same
  path are similar enough to be paired by it: a second step written from the
  template was reported as the first's rename, so `record-integrity` compared
  it with its *sibling's* blob and refused a valid record. A relocation removes
  its source; a file still sitting there was never moved. `stepRecordOrigin`
  asks the narrower question where the followed source still exists.
- **ADR-004 decision 4, at the root.** `withAnotherPathsUnitMergedIn` lands
  another path's completed unit on the remote trunk through its own `--no-ff`
  merge, with `done` recorded in a commit after it — the shape S06's rule
  requires of everyone — moves the local trunk to match, and merges it into the
  path branch. `publishedRepository` and `readyRepository` call it, so every
  fixture built on them carries the merge and a fixture written later has it
  without being told.
- **The fixture repositories became conforming ones**: the step record carries
  its self-review and its review, and so does the pilot's, because a harness
  that installs a repository the checker would refuse is not a baseline.
- **The catalogue, the matrix, the soundness note and the module note** say
  what the twenty-six rules are and what `review` does not read.
